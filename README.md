**What makes a startup idea good?** A startup idea is good when a specific buyer already feels a painful, repeated problem, the market around it is moving, and a small team can reach the first customers before the money runs out. This list scores ideas on market movement (funded-startup momentum and fresh accelerator cohorts), survival, scale and what independent products in the space actually earn.

**What is a startup?** A startup is a young company built to find a repeatable, scalable business model. Most are software or technology companies, and many are funded by accelerators or venture investors, but a bootstrapped product that earns its own revenue counts too.

Ranked from 17,000+ funded startups (scored for momentum and attractiveness, with 5,900+ Y Combinator batch records for the time series), 30,000+ companies in the Stripe Index, 13,000+ indie products tracked with revenue, 690+ software categories reviewed, 40,000+ feature requests, 7,000+ AI agent connectors and 1M+ real customer complaints.

## Legend

* 🚀 gaining share: the space's share of 2024 to 2026 YC batches is 1.5x or more its 2019 to 2021 share
* 🧊 cooling: the share fell to two thirds or less of its 2019 to 2021 level
* 💵 1 in 10+ independent products in the matching category clear $1K a month
* 🧩 underfunded: many paying operators per funded startup
* ⚠️ 9%+ of funded companies in the space are marked inactive
* 🧪 small sample (under 50 products), treat as indicative

## How to pick and test a startup idea

Seven steps, in order. Stop at the first one that fails.

1. **Name the buyer and the repeated problem.** One sentence: who pays, for which job, how often it hurts. If you cannot write it, you have a topic, not an idea.
2. **Check the market is moving.** Compare the space's share of recent YC batches with 2019 to 2021. A rising share (🚀) means investors and talent are arriving. A falling share (🧊) means you need a sharper wedge. AI agents went from 3.2% to 26.3%.
3. **Count the funded competition.** 1,000+ funded startups in a space means VC-backed rivals. Under 300 means room, but check that buyers exist.
4. **Count the payers.** Look for 100+ real businesses already paying in the space. The Stripe Index is a quick check. A high ratio of paying businesses to funded startups is a sign of underserved demand.
5. **Check what independents earn.** If under 1 in 10 products in the category clear $1K a month, plan a narrow wedge. Across 13,000+ tracked products, 9.5% do.
6. **Find the repeated complaint.** One angry review is noise. The same complaint across 8+ products rated 4 out of 5 for severity is a gap.
7. **Sell before you build.** Get 10 people in the niche to pre-pay, join a paid pilot or book a call about the problem. Ten refusals are a result too. Set a kill date before you start.

**Quick scorecard.** Score each 0 or 1: rising or flat batch share, under 1,000 funded rivals, 100+ paying businesses, category above 5% earning $1K+, a complaint across 8+ products, 10 pre-orders. 5 or 6 means build. 3 or 4 means narrow the niche. 0 to 2 means move on.

**Startup idea checklist before you quit your job:**

* You can name 20 real buyers and have spoken to 10.
* The problem repeats at least weekly for the buyer.
* You can reach the buyer without paid ads.
* The first version takes under 8 weeks to build.
* Someone has already paid for a workaround (a freelancer, a spreadsheet, a consultant).

## Glossary

* **Startup** - A young company built to find a repeatable, scalable business model.
* **Bootstrapping** - Funding a company from customer revenue and personal savings instead of outside investors.
* **MRR** - Monthly recurring revenue: subscription revenue normalized to one month.
* **ARR** - Annual recurring revenue: MRR times 12.
* **Accelerator** - A program that invests a small amount in a batch of startups in exchange for equity and coaching.
* **Batch** - One cohort of companies admitted to an accelerator in the same period.
* **Pre-seed and seed** - The earliest funding rounds, usually before or just after the first product.
* **Series A** - The first large venture round, usually raised once there is repeatable traction.
* **Venture-scale** - A market big enough that investors expect a very large outcome.
* **Momentum score** - A 1 to 10 BigIdeasDB score for how fast a funded startup's space and traction appear to be moving.
* **B2B** - Business-to-business: selling to companies, not consumers.
* **Vertical startup** - A company built for one industry's workflow.
* **Wedge** - The narrow first product or customer a startup uses to enter a crowded market.
* **Micro SaaS** - A small software product, often run by one person, serving one narrow niche.
* **Agentic AI** - Software that takes multi-step actions on a user's behalf instead of only answering questions.
* **Pre-sell** - Collecting payment or a signed pilot before the product is built.
* **Churn** - The share of customers or revenue lost in a period.
* **ICP** - Ideal customer profile: the type of buyer a product is built for.

### What are good startup ideas for students?

Pick a consumer-facing product you can build alone and sell without sales calls. Among indie products, habit, health and fitness apps have the highest share earning anything at 59%. Aim for a first $100 a month in a category with a high buildability score, and talk to 10 buyers before you code.

### What are scalable business ideas?

Scalable business ideas use recurring or usage-based pricing so revenue grows faster than cost. Evals, observability and cost control for AI teams leads the scalable list with 120+ funded startups and an attractiveness score of 7.2. Of 13,000+ tracked indie products, 330+ reach $10K a month.

### Can you start a startup without venture capital?

Yes. 5,700+ of 13,000+ tracked independent products earn revenue, 1,300+ clear $1K a month and 330+ clear $10K a month. Newsletter and creator tools, AI image and video generators and SEO tools lead the share earning $1K, at 12% to 13%.

### Which startup sectors are cooling?

Merchant tools for storefront operators cooled most. Its share of YC batches fell from 10.6% in 2019 to 2021 to 2.5% in 2024 to 2026. Marketplaces, payments, ecommerce tools and lending also lost share. Cooling does not mean dead, but easy VC enthusiasm has passed and a sharper wedge is required.

### Which markets are underfunded but have real demand?

By paying businesses per funded startup, home trades and services leads: 990+ paying businesses in the Stripe Index against 180+ funded startups. Nonprofits and fundraising and ecommerce and online stores follow. That is demand without a crowd of venture-backed rivals.

### How do you pick a startup idea?

Pick a startup idea by checking five things in order: a named buyer with a repeated problem, a market whose share of new startups is flat or rising, fewer than 1,000 funded rivals, 100+ businesses already paying in the space, and a complaint repeated across 8+ products. Then pre-sell to 10 buyers before building.

### How do you validate a startup idea?

Validate a startup idea by selling before you build. Interview 10 buyers, ask 3 to pre-pay or sign a pilot, and check the market with data: funded competitors, paying businesses and what independents earn. Only 9.5% of 13,000+ tracked indie products clear $1K a month, so evidence matters.
